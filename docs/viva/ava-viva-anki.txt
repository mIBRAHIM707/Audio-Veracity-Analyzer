Q4. Can a 300-million-parameter model really run in real time on a CPU, with no GPU?	<b>Answer (say this):</b> Yes, if we keep only part of it. A 2026 study ran a 7-layer version of this kind of model on a single CPU core inside a browser plugin. We keep about 7 to 8 of the 24 transformer layers, because earlier work shows deepfake cues sit in the lower layers, and compress the weights to INT8 (8-bit integers), which typically gives a 2 to 3 times speed-up. Our target is one 4-second window scored in under 1.5 seconds on an 8-core, 32 GB server, and 8 simultaneous calls per box, with no GPU (graphics processing unit). Q9. Why is this a Complex Computing Problem and not just a software build?	<b>Answer (say this):</b> It has conflicting requirements with no textbook answer: detection accuracy against CPU-only real-time speed against zero internet, on 8 kHz phone audio that destroys the high-frequency cues most detectors rely on. Q13. Punjabi has many accents. Won't the model flag an unfamiliar accent as fake?	<b>Answer (say this):</b> Accent is not what AVA listens for. The model looks for traces AI generators leave behind (unnatural timing, phase and texture), which are properties of the generator; accent is a property of the human speaker. It can only learn "accent equals fake" if an accent appears on one side only, so every fake is made from the same people we recorded, and voice conversion keeps their original accent, meaning every accent sits in both real and fake. The real risk is a false alarm on an accent we never recorded, so we do not guess: we hold out test-only dialects (Jhangvi, Shahpuri) and report the false-alarm rate per dialect. Nobody has published that for Urdu or Punjabi, so the measurement itself is a contribution.<br><br><b>Source:</b> brief §2, §4 (B), §7<br><br><b>Trap:</b> Do not claim "we cover all Punjabi accents". Q15. Why a 4-second window? A fraudster can say "reset my PIN" in less than that.	<b>Answer (say this):</b> Because shorter windows roughly double the error rate, and 4 seconds is what these detectors are trained and benchmarked on. We score the last 4 seconds every 2 seconds, so the score updates continuously, and we wait for two windows, about 6 seconds of speech, before the first verdict, which avoids false alarms on "hello, how are you". Fraud is not one sentence; the caller has to get through identity checks, so a first verdict within 6 seconds and updates every 2 seconds is in time. How fast we can be confident is research question A: we plot seconds-to-alert against accuracy over GSM and AMR phone audio, and the dashboard highlights which seconds of a call were fake, because listeners miss a single fake sentence 77% of the time.<br><br><b>Source:</b> brief §2, §4 (A, D), §5; PRD FR-I4 to FR-I6, §4.1; ADR-11<br><br><b>Trap:</b> Do not say "under 150 ms" (wrong claim 6). Q16. How do you make sure the model is not just memorising voices?	<b>Answer (say this):</b> Speaker-disjoint splits: 70% train, 10% dev, 20% test, split by person, so no speaker appears in two splits, and a script checks that before every run. Otherwise, with few speakers, the model can pass by learning "this voice is always fake", and the number collapses on new callers, exactly the people the product faces. The same logic is why every consenting speaker appears on both the real and the fake side: the model cannot use "whose voice is this" as a shortcut. It is also why we match silence and length across real and fake clips, since one published model cheated by measuring silence, and why we never use WhatsApp voice notes for real clips only, or the model learns "compressed equals real".<br><br><b>Source:</b> brief §6.4, §6.6, §6.8; PRD A4, FR-D7, §4.1<br><br><b>Trap:</b> Do not say "we use a random split".<br><br><b>Follow-up they may ask:</b> How many speakers end up in the test set?	ava viva engineering_knowledge speaker_disjoint evaluation likely

Q19. What happens when AVA flags a real customer as fake?	<b>Answer (say this):</b> Nothing happens to the call. AVA is passive and advisory: it listens to a copy of the audio, never sits in the call path, and never blocks or ends a call; a human analyst sees the alert and decides. We smooth the scores with a moving average and use separate on and off thresholds, for example enter "alert" at 0.75 and leave it at 0.55, so one noisy 4-second window cannot flip an alarm on and off. Thresholds are configurable because the right trade-off between a missed fraud and an annoyed customer differs between a bank and a call centre. And we measure the false-alarm rate per dialect, so an operator knows where the model is weakest.<br><br><b>Source:</b> PRD §2.2 (non-goals), FR-T5, FR-I7, §4.1; architecture §2.4, ADR-11; brief §4 (B), §12<br><br><b>Trap:</b> Do not say "AVA is accurate enough that this won't happen".
